Tutorial BB: Hex Bolt Drawing

Source

Ming C. Leu, Wenjin Tao, Amir Ghazanfari and Krishna Kolan, NX 12 for Engineering Design (Missouri University of Science and Technology), section 5.6 (example).
You learn

a large sheet at an enlarging scale, Projected View, hidden lines, vertical dimensions, a Note with a leader and symbols, Radial, an isometric Base View
Part

tutorial AQ's screw, Impeller_hexa-bolt. The replay runs AQ first.
Sizes

the book’s, in inches, typed in millimetres.

5.7, the last section of the chapter, is an open exercise: draft the chapter 4 parts. It is not replayed; tutorials AZ and BB cover the commands it would use.

Step by step

1. Sheet and views

Open Impeller_hexa-bolt, press Ctrl+Shift+D and click New Sheet. Choose the template ANSIE_Landscape.svg (E, 34 x 44 in), the scale 8 and Third angle.

Select the screw’s bodies and click Base View, direction Front: the screw stands with its head up. Select the view, click Projected View, then Right and Above, and press MB2. The view above is the top view: the hexagon. Display Borders is already off, as in tutorial AZ.

Select the three views and turn on their Hard Hidden: hidden lines are drawn dashed. The top view now shows the shank under the head as a dashed circle.

2. Dimensions

Pick two vertices and click Vertical:

3. The thread note

Select an edge of the thread on the front view and click Note. Type Right Hand, click the Ø button, type 0.20 x 1.5, press Enter, type Pitch 0.05, Angle 60 and click the ° button. Set the lettering height to 7 mm and the stub length to 12.7 mm, and press MB2. The leader runs from the thread to the note.

4. Radial and the isometric view

Select the circle on top of the head in the top view and click Radial. Select the screw’s bodies again, click Base View, and set the direction to isometric.

Save the part.

The views' directions are checked, and the top view must show a hidden circle of the shank’s radius. Each dimension is compared with the screw’s own geometry: its height, the head, the step, and where the head’s sphere meets the hexagon. It must also match the book’s number to its three decimals. The note’s arrow is traced back from the sheet into the model and must end on the threaded shank.

What differs from NX

  • As in tutorial AZ, the sheet’s size, scale and projection are properties, and a view’s direction is a property.

  • NX sets the hidden line style in the views' Settings dialog. TechDraw’s Hard Hidden draws hidden lines dashed.

  • NX drags the leader from the shaft and sets the lettering height and leader length in Settings afterwards. Here they are fields of the Note dialog.

  • The note’s text is the book’s. It gives Ø0.20, while AQ’s thread is the book’s 1/4-20 UNC on a Ø0.25 shank.

  • The book’s figure labels the circle on top of the head Ø0.200, which is the size of its radius (0.201). TechDraw’s Radial shows it as a radius.

  • TechDraw computes hidden lines for the detailed thread, so these views take longer to appear than plain ones.
