= Modeling
:description: Building parts in the Modeling application: the feature workflow, datums, patterns, design changes, materials and mass.

The *Modeling* application is FreeCAD's PartDesign workbench with NX's ribbon.
kbd:[Ctrl+M] switches to it from any other application.

image::tutorial-E-saddle-bracket.png[The Saddle Bracket in Modeling, features renamed in the Part Navigator]

== A new part

kbd:[Ctrl+N] works like NX's *Model* template: the new part already has a Body, ready to sketch on.
Units are millimetres and kilograms (FreeCAD's Standard schema), whatever FreeCAD itself was set to; see `units` in xref:settings.adoc[].

The part gets its name at the first save (kbd:[Ctrl+S]).
Files are FreeCAD `.FCStd` files, not `.prt`.

== The feature workflow

The Home tab's *Feature* group works the NX way:

. Start the feature: kbd:[X] for *Extrude*, or *Revolve*, *Hole*, *Extrude (Subtract)*, *Revolve (Subtract)*.
. With nothing selected, pick a plane or a face; a sketch opens on it.
  With a face or datum plane already selected, it opens there at once; with a sketch selected, the feature starts from that sketch.
  To use curves of an existing sketch instead, pick them (<<sections>>).
. Draw the section (xref:sketching.adoc[]).
. kbd:[Ctrl+Q] (*Finish Sketch*) carries straight on into the feature's dialog.
. Type the values and press *MB2* (or *OK*).

== Extrude [[extrude]]

Extrude works the same however it is started: kbd:[X], the ribbon, the Command Finder, the radial menu, or *Edit Parameters* on an existing extrude.
Its dialog has two parts.
At the top, the NX command box shows the section, the body and the *Boolean*.
Below it is FreeCAD's own Pad or Pocket panel, where the limits, the direction and the draft are typed.

image::tutorial-AE-02-unite.png[The Extrude dialog: the NX command box above FreeCAD's Pad panel]

=== Boolean

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| NX Boolean | What NX-Style builds

| *None* (a new body)
| A Pad that starts a solid. In a Body that has no solid yet, the Pad goes into that Body. Otherwise a new Body is made, with a sub-shape binder of the sketch, and the Pad goes in there. That Pad's custom direction is set to the sketch normal, because the binder's face can point the other way. The active Body does not change, so later extrudes still go to the body you were working on.

| *Unite*
| A Pad in the Body that holds the sketch.

| *Subtract*
| A Pocket in the Body that holds the sketch.

| *Intersect*
| Not supported. It is in the list, but greyed out, and choosing it gives the reason in the dialog. NX-Style does not approximate it.
|===

The Boolean starts as NX infers it: *Unite* when the sketch's Body already has a solid, otherwise *None* (a new body). The Boolean names follow NX 2212, as in Shih's NX 2212 exercises.
*Extrude (Subtract)* starts with *Subtract* chosen.
Changing the Boolean replaces the preview Pad with a Pocket, or the reverse; the length typed so far is kept.
Unite and Subtract always work on the Body that holds the sketch. NX lets you pick another target body; NX-Style does not.

NOTE: *Subtract* cuts into the part, against the sketch normal, unless *Reversed* is ticked.
NX keeps the same direction for every Boolean, so there you reverse it.

=== Errors, OK and Cancel

The command box's last line shows the state of the extrude.

* An error leaves *OK* unavailable, and MB2 does nothing.
  For example: FreeCAD cannot build the feature from these values, or a Unite misses the part and would leave a separate solid (NX: “tool body does not intersect the target”), or Unite or Subtract is chosen when there is no solid to work on.
* A warning does not stop OK.
  For example: a Subtract that removes nothing, or a number the field read differently from what was typed. With a comma as the decimal separator, `3.175` is read as 3175 mm.
* *OK* (or MB2) adds one undo step, named *Extrude*. Editing an extrude adds one named *Edit Extrude*.
* *Cancel* or kbd:[Esc] removes the preview, and with it any new Body or binder. Visibility and the selection go back to how they were, and nothing is added to the undo list.
  The sketch drawn inside the command stays, as its own undo steps.
* While the dialog is open, the Type Filter is switched off so the command can pick what it needs. It comes back afterwards.

=== Not supported yet

These NX Extrude options are not offered, and the command box lists them:

* Intersect;
* choosing the target body;
* a start limit away from the sketch plane on one side (FreeCAD's *Two sided* gives a length each way);
* Until Extended;
* draft from the section, asymmetric or with matched ends (FreeCAD has a single taper angle);
* NX's section Offset (FreeCAD's Offset belongs to Up to face);
* body type Sheet;
* changing the Boolean while editing an extrude. Delete it and extrude again;
* a section of curves from several sketches, or of edges of a solid.

Hole still opens FreeCAD's own dialog directly. It does not have the command box yet.

=== Unite and subtract between bodies

*Unite / Subtract* in the Feature group combines separate bodies.

=== Extrude limits

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| NX | FreeCAD (in the Extrude dialog)

| Value | Dimension
| Start 0, End d, Reverse Direction | Dimension d, *Reversed* ticked (the book's −ZC 0.5 in)
| Symmetric Value | Symmetric (the value is the total width, as in NX since NX 1926)
| Start and End limits on both sides | Two sided (a length each way)
| Until Next | To first
| Until Selected | Up to face
| Through All | Through all
|===

NOTE: *To first* and *Up to face* into a hole stop 1 mm into the part in FreeCAD 1.1.4 (xref:known-issues.adoc[]).
xref:tutorials/l.adoc[Tutorial L] shows the workaround.

== Sections [[sections]]

Extrude and Revolve take their section as NX's *Section* collector does: a whole sketch, or some curves of one sketch.
The *Curve Rule* on the Top Border Bar, next to the Selection Scope, says what one pick adds.

* *Inferred Curves* (the default) takes the whole sketch, as before.
* *Single Curve* takes the picked curve.
* *Tangent Curves* and *Connected Curves* take its chain (xref:selection.adoc[]); Connected stops where three curves meet.

The curves can be picked before the command, or after it starts.
With nothing selected the command asks for a sketch plane, as before; pick a sketch curve instead, and FreeCAD's plane pick closes and the dialog opens with that curve.
Every further pick adds to the section, and Ctrl+MB1 on a picked curve takes it out.
A plain click on a picked curve changes nothing: FreeCAD would select the whole sketch there, and only *Inferred Curves* takes the whole sketch.
The section's curves stay selected, so they show which they are, and the command box counts them (*Select Curve (10)*); its tooltip names them.
Where a solid's face or edge lies on a curve, hold MB1 for QuickPick and choose the sketch's curve.

The feature's Profile is those curves only, so one sketch can hold several loops that share curves or overlap, as the impeller casing of tutorial AG does.
Until the curves close, the command box says where the section is open or branches, and OK is unavailable.
A sketch used in part stays shown after OK, because its other curves are still to be used; a whole sketch is hidden, as before.
A section of a new body (Boolean *None* when the sketch's Body has a solid) is a binder of the picked curves only.

FreeCAD names a curve after the sketch's shape, not after its geometry number: outside the sketch, the first line drawn need not be *Edge1*.

== Revolve [[revolve]]

Revolve has the same command box as Extrude, over FreeCAD's Revolution panel (Groove for *Subtract*).
*Revolve (Subtract)* starts with *Subtract* chosen; *Intersect* is shown and refused.
OK adds one undo step, named *Revolve*, and Cancel or kbd:[Esc] leaves nothing behind.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| NX | What NX-Style builds

| *Specify Vector* XC, YC, ZC through *Specify Point* (0, 0, 0)
| The Body's own X, Y or Z axis. −XC, −YC and −ZC tick *Reversed*.

| A vector through another point
| A datum line through that point, which the Revolve keeps (Cancel removes it).

| *Sketch H axis*, *Sketch V axis*, *Line…*
| The sketch's axis, or the sketch line picked after *Line…*. *Sketch V axis* is the default, as in FreeCAD.

| *Limits* Start 0, End 180
| *Angle* 180. With the Start on the other side of the sketch plane (Start −30, End 120): *Two angles*, 120 and 30.

| Start and End on one side of the sketch plane, neither 0
| Not supported: FreeCAD revolves from the sketch plane. The command box says so and OK is unavailable.
|===

The box drives FreeCAD's panel: what it sets shows there, and what is typed there shows in the box.
A new axis rebuilds the preview, because FreeCAD's panel takes an axis only from its list.
When editing a Revolve, change its axis in FreeCAD's axis list; the Boolean cannot change either.

== Datums

*Feature › Datum* has Datum Plane, Datum Axis, Point and Datum CSYS.

* The Body has its own origin (XY, XZ and YZ planes and the axes). NX-Style keeps it hidden and FreeCAD shows it while you pick a sketch plane.
* *At Angle* datum planes rotate about the face's own axes in the attachment offset; NX lets you pick the hinge axis (xref:tutorials/c.adoc[Tutorial C]).
* Datum CSYS with nothing selected asks whether to continue without an attachment; NX places it at the WCS (xref:tutorials/e.adoc[Tutorial E]).

== Holes

*Hole* drills at the circle centres of a sketch on a face.
Select the face (or a datum plane), click *Hole*, place the centres in the sketch with kbd:[O] and kbd:[D], and kbd:[Ctrl+Q] continues into the Hole dialog.
NX places a hole by its point and adds the location dimensions by itself.

== Edge Blend, Chamfer, Draft, Shell

These are FreeCAD's Fillet, Chamfer, Draft and Thickness.
Select the edges or faces first, then the command.

*Edge Blend* also works the other way round: start it with nothing selected and it opens a selection collector; pick the edges (*Tangent Curves* takes a whole smooth chain from one pick), then OK hands them to the Fillet for the radius.
It refuses what it cannot round (a face, an edge of another body) and says why (xref:selection.adoc#_commands_that_collect_edge_blend[]).

TIP: In FreeCAD 1.1.4, *Shell* can fail after *Edge Blend* on the same edges.
Shell first, then blend (xref:tutorials/f.adoc[Tutorial F]).

== Patterns and mirror

NX has one *Pattern Feature* with a Layout list; FreeCAD has separate buttons.

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| NX layout | Button

| Linear | *Pattern Feature* (FreeCAD's Linear Pattern); Direction 2 is a tick box in the same dialog. NX's negative pitch is FreeCAD's spacing with *Reverse*.
| Circular | *Circular Pattern*; the axis is chosen from a list in the dialog.
| Several at once | *Multi-Transform*
|===

*Mirror Feature*: select the feature first, then start the command.
Inside a Body the mirror unites by itself.

== Design changes

* *Edit Parameters* reopens the feature's dialog. It is on the ribbon, the radial menu, and the MB3 menu of the feature in the Part Navigator.
  An extrude opens in the Extrude dialog with its command box (<<extrude>>), so *Cancel* puts every value back.
  Double-clicking the feature opens FreeCAD's own dialog without the command box.
  NX also lets you edit a dimension shown in the graphics window; here the change goes through the dialog or the sketch.
* kbd:[F2] or MB3 › *Rename* in the Part Navigator gives a feature a name.
* *Make Current Feature* rolls the part back to that feature; later features wait until you make the last one current again.
* *Suppress* switches a feature off and on again (NX's Suppress and Unsuppress).
  FreeCAD keeps the feature's links in its properties; xref:tutorials/m.adoc[Tutorial M] shows parent/child behaviour.
* *Update Model* recomputes.

Later features follow a change, including a fillet added to the first sketch afterwards (xref:tutorials/e.adoc[Tutorial E]).

== Expressions

kbd:[Ctrl+E] opens the *Expressions* sheet: a FreeCAD spreadsheet named `Expressions`, with names in one column and values in the next.
A dimension uses a name as `=Expressions.L`.
Change the value in the sheet and the part follows (xref:tutorials/k.adoc[Tutorial K]).

FreeCAD refuses names that are unit symbols: the newton `N` becomes `N_holes`, for example.

== Materials and mass

. Select the body and click *Render › Material › Assign Material*.
  Pick the material; the choice applies at once, and the dialog closes with *Close*.
. *Analysis › Measure › Measure Bodies* shows volume, surface area, the material and density, mass and centre of mass in an Information window, in mm³, mm² and kg.

FreeCAD's own Measure has no mass; *Measure Bodies* is NX-Style's.
FreeCAD 1.1 also applies the material's appearance, so a steel body renders nearly black.

== Associative copy, surfaces, synchronous modeling

* *WAVE Linker*, *Extract Geometry* and *Clone Body* are FreeCAD's sub-shape binder, shape binder and clone.
* The *Surface* tab holds Through Curves, Swept, N-Sided Surface and the sheet operations from FreeCAD's Part and Surface workbenches.
* *Synchronous Modeling* is limited to *Delete Face* (FreeCAD's Defeaturing).

The full list of buttons is in xref:nx-to-freecad.adoc[].
