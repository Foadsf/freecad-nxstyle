= Modeling
:description: Building parts in the Modeling application: the feature workflow, datums, patterns, design changes, materials and mass.

The *Modeling* application is FreeCAD's PartDesign workbench with NX's ribbon.
kbd:[Ctrl+M] switches to it from any other application.

image::tutorial-E-saddle-bracket.png[The Saddle Bracket in Modeling, features renamed in the Part Navigator]

== A new part

kbd:[Ctrl+N] works like NX's *Model* template: the new part already has a Body, ready to sketch on.
Units are millimetres and kilograms (FreeCAD's Standard schema), whatever FreeCAD itself was set to; see `units` in xref:settings.adoc[].

The part gets its name at the first save (kbd:[Ctrl+S]).
Files are FreeCAD `.FCStd` files, not `.prt`.

== The feature workflow

The Home tab's *Feature* group works the NX way:

. Start the feature: kbd:[X] for *Extrude*, or *Revolve*, *Hole*, *Extrude (Subtract)*, *Revolve (Subtract)*.
. With nothing selected, pick a plane or a face; a sketch opens on it.
  With a face or datum plane already selected, it opens there at once; with a sketch selected, the feature starts from that sketch.
. Draw the section (xref:sketching.adoc[]).
. kbd:[Ctrl+Q] (*Finish Sketch*) carries straight on into the feature's dialog.
. Type the values and press *MB2* (or *OK*).

=== Unite and subtract

NX's Extrude has a Boolean option; FreeCAD's has not.

* Features in one Body unite by themselves: an Extrude onto the part is NX's Extrude with *Unite*.
* *Extrude (Subtract)* and *Revolve (Subtract)* are their own buttons (FreeCAD's Pocket and Groove).
  They cut into the part by default, so NX's *Reverse Direction* is rarely needed.
* *Unite / Subtract* in the Feature group combines separate bodies.

=== Extrude limits

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| NX | FreeCAD (in the Extrude dialog)

| Value | Dimension
| Symmetric Value | Symmetric (the value is the total width, as in NX since NX 1926)
| Start and End limits on both sides | Two sided (a length each way)
| Until Next | To first
| Until Selected | Up to face
| Through All | Through all
|===

NOTE: *To first* and *Up to face* into a hole stop 1 mm into the part in FreeCAD 1.1.4 (xref:known-issues.adoc[]).
xref:tutorials/l.adoc[Tutorial L] shows the workaround.

== Datums

*Feature › Datum* has Datum Plane, Datum Axis, Point and Datum CSYS.

* The Body has its own origin (XY, XZ and YZ planes and the axes). NX-Style keeps it hidden and FreeCAD shows it while you pick a sketch plane.
* *At Angle* datum planes rotate about the face's own axes in the attachment offset; NX lets you pick the hinge axis (xref:tutorials/c.adoc[Tutorial C]).
* Datum CSYS with nothing selected asks whether to continue without an attachment; NX places it at the WCS (xref:tutorials/e.adoc[Tutorial E]).

== Holes

*Hole* drills at the circle centres of a sketch on a face.
Select the face (or a datum plane), click *Hole*, place the centres in the sketch with kbd:[O] and kbd:[D], and kbd:[Ctrl+Q] continues into the Hole dialog.
NX places a hole by its point and adds the location dimensions by itself.

== Edge Blend, Chamfer, Draft, Shell

These are FreeCAD's Fillet, Chamfer, Draft and Thickness.
Select the edges or faces first, then the command.

*Edge Blend* also works the other way round: start it with nothing selected and it opens a selection collector; pick the edges (*Tangent Curves* takes a whole smooth chain from one pick), then OK hands them to the Fillet for the radius.
It refuses what it cannot round (a face, an edge of another body) and says why (xref:selection.adoc#_commands_that_collect_edge_blend[]).

TIP: In FreeCAD 1.1.4, *Shell* can fail after *Edge Blend* on the same edges.
Shell first, then blend (xref:tutorials/f.adoc[Tutorial F]).

== Patterns and mirror

NX has one *Pattern Feature* with a Layout list; FreeCAD has separate buttons.

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| NX layout | Button

| Linear | *Pattern Feature* (FreeCAD's Linear Pattern); Direction 2 is a tick box in the same dialog. NX's negative pitch is FreeCAD's spacing with *Reverse*.
| Circular | *Circular Pattern*; the axis is chosen from a list in the dialog.
| Several at once | *Multi-Transform*
|===

*Mirror Feature*: select the feature first, then start the command.
Inside a Body the mirror unites by itself.

== Design changes

* *Edit Parameters* (or double-click the feature in the Part Navigator) reopens the feature's dialog.
  NX also lets you edit a dimension shown in the graphics window; here the change goes through the dialog or the sketch.
* kbd:[F2] or MB3 › *Rename* in the Part Navigator gives a feature a name.
* *Make Current Feature* rolls the part back to that feature; later features wait until you make the last one current again.
* *Suppress* switches a feature off and on again (NX's Suppress and Unsuppress).
  FreeCAD keeps the feature's links in its properties; xref:tutorials/m.adoc[Tutorial M] shows parent/child behaviour.
* *Update Model* recomputes.

Later features follow a change, including a fillet added to the first sketch afterwards (xref:tutorials/e.adoc[Tutorial E]).

== Expressions

kbd:[Ctrl+E] opens the *Expressions* sheet: a FreeCAD spreadsheet named `Expressions`, with names in one column and values in the next.
A dimension uses a name as `=Expressions.L`.
Change the value in the sheet and the part follows (xref:tutorials/k.adoc[Tutorial K]).

FreeCAD refuses names that are unit symbols: the newton `N` becomes `N_holes`, for example.

== Materials and mass

. Select the body and click *Render › Material › Assign Material*.
  Pick the material; the choice applies at once, and the dialog closes with *Close*.
. *Analysis › Measure › Measure Bodies* shows volume, surface area, the material and density, mass and centre of mass in an Information window, in mm³, mm² and kg.

FreeCAD's own Measure has no mass; *Measure Bodies* is NX-Style's.
FreeCAD 1.1 also applies the material's appearance, so a steel body renders nearly black.

== Associative copy, surfaces, synchronous modeling

* *WAVE Linker*, *Extract Geometry* and *Clone Body* are FreeCAD's sub-shape binder, shape binder and clone.
* The *Surface* tab holds Through Curves, Swept, N-Sided Surface and the sheet operations from FreeCAD's Part and Surface workbenches.
* *Synchronous Modeling* is limited to *Delete Face* (FreeCAD's Defeaturing).

The full list of buttons is in xref:nx-to-freecad.adoc[].
