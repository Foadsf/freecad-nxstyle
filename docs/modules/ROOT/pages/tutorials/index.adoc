= Tutorials
:description: NX textbook exercises and Grasshopper video tutorials, step by step in FreeCAD NX-Style, with what is the same and what differs.
include::partial$generated/tutorial-meta.adoc[]

Tutorials A to N are exercises from NX, GD&T and engineering-graphics textbooks, done in NX-Style the way the book tells an NX user to work.
Tutorials O to R are Grasshopper video tutorials done on the xref:algorithmic-modeling.adoc[Algorithmic Modeling] canvas (Katydid).
Tutorials S to AC are precision-engineering work: a tolerance stack-up checked against the book's numbers, a part documented to ISO GPS in 3D, volume claims and assembly clearance in a system layout, a release with an engineering change, a sheet metal bracket whose flat pattern is checked by hand, an opto-mechanical pin location, PMI organised in model views, the model with its PMI exchanged through STEP AP242, a composite position frame evaluated from CMM data, the measurement capability of the inspection methods, and a drawing whose title block comes from the release.
Tutorial AD joins a laser-cut tray with tabs and slots and lays its parts flat for the cutter.
Tutorials AF to AK are the sketching examples and exercises of _NX 12 for Engineering Design_: a sketch constrained to size and extruded, a casing sketch built with Move Curve copies, an impeller blade of tangent arcs, a slotted circular base, and a holder with lugs copied round a bolt circle.
Every page lists the original steps beside the NX-Style steps, so you can follow along with the book or the video, or without them.

The step tables are not written by hand.
The xref:testing.adoc#tutorial-replay[tutorial replay] presses the same keys, clicks the same NX-named ribbon buttons, clicks sketch points with MB1 and accepts dialogs with MB2, places canvas components by typing their names and drags wires between grips, then checks each result by its volume.
The tables show its last runs on FreeCAD {replay-freecad}: {replay-steps} steps.

== Before you start

* Install and start NX-Style (xref:installation.adoc[]), and try xref:quick-start.adoc[] first.
* Parts are metric. The books work in inches; their sizes are typed in millimetres (2.5 in = 63.5 mm).
* Where a book gives a size only in a figure, the tutorial page says so and gives the size used here.

== The books

* Randy H. Shih, _Parametric Modeling with Siemens NX_ (SDC Publications): tutorials A, C–G, I–N and W (ch. 13, sheet metal).
* Gene R. Cogorno, _Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing for Mechanical Design_, 3rd ed. (McGraw-Hill): tutorial H.
* James Leake and Jacob Borgerson, _Engineering Design Graphics_ (Wiley): tutorial K.
* Tutorial B is a synthesised first-part exercise.
* Bryan R. Fischer, _Mechanical Tolerance Stackup and Analysis_, 2nd ed. (Taylor & Francis): tutorial S, and the stack-up method.
* Andreas Wünsch, Fabian Pilz and Sándor Vajna, _Siemens NX für Einsteiger – kurz und bündig_, 4th ed., section 6.10: the PMI workflow of tutorial T and the model views of tutorial Y.
* Sham Tickoo, _Siemens NX 12.0 for Designers_, ch. 12: the default model views, tutorial Y.
* Paul J. Drake, _Dimensioning and Tolerancing Handbook_ (McGraw-Hill), ch. 6 (ASME and ISO compared), ch. 9 (statistical tolerancing) and ch. 24 (pinned interfaces, tutorial X); Gene R. Cogorno (above), ch. 3, 7, 8 and 13: the checks, the fastener formulas and the gauge sizes.
* Fischer (above), ch. 15: the lever of an angle error, tutorial X.
* Cogorno (above), ch. 12 and 13: composite position and its graphic analysis, tutorial AA. Drake (above), ch. 20 and 25: measurement systems and the gauge study example, tutorial AB; ch. 4: the title and revision blocks, tutorial AC.
* The PLM books of John Stark, Michael Grieves, and Antti Saaksvuori and Anselmi Immonen: release and engineering change, tutorial V.
* Tutorial U's layout is ours; Assembly Clearance follows NX's.
* Tutorial AE is ours: the acceptance case of the Extrude command (None for a new body, Unite, Subtract, edit, cancel, undo, reopen).
* Ming C. Leu, Wenjin Tao, Amir Ghazanfari and Krishna Kolan, _NX 12 for Engineering Design_ (Missouri University of Science and Technology), sections 3.5 and 3.6: tutorials AF, AG, AH, AJ and AK.
* ISO values (ISO 286 limits, ISO 2768 general tolerances) were checked against two published tables each; the unit tests also compare them with the ISO 286-1 formulas.
