= Tutorial AO: Impeller Lower Casing, Extrude and Revolve
:description: The casing sketch of tutorial AG made solid: its wall revolved 180° about XC from curves picked one by one, its edge extruded 0.5 along -ZC and mirrored, and its flange revolved (NX 12 for Engineering Design, 4.4).

[horizontal]
Source:: Ming C. Leu, Wenjin Tao, Amir Ghazanfari and Krishna Kolan, _NX 12 for Engineering Design_ (Missouri University of Science and Technology), section 4.4 (Swept Features). It continues the casing sketch of section 3.5.2, xref:tutorials/ag.adoc[tutorial AG].
You learn:: the Curve Rule (Single Curve), Revolve with Specify Vector, Specify Point and the angle limits, Extrude of picked curves as a body of its own, QuickPick for curves that lie on a solid, Mirror Feature
Sizes:: the book's, in inches, typed in millimetres. The wall and the flange revolved about XC through (0, 0, 0) from 0 to 180°. The edge extruded 0.5 (12.7 mm) along -ZC.

The replay runs tutorial AG first and opens the part it saved.

== Step by step

=== 1. The wall

Open `Impeller_lower_casing`, the part of tutorial AG.
Click *Revolve*.
FreeCAD first offers its list of sketch planes; you don't need it, because picking a curve of the sketch closes the list and opens the Revolve dialog with that curve.

Set the Curve Rule on the Top Border Bar (beside the Selection Filter) to *Single Curve*.
The default, Inferred Curves, would take the whole sketch, which is not one closed profile.
Click the 10 curves of the wall: the inner curve's four, the four of the curve 0.5 above it, and the two short lines that close its ends.

In the dialog choose *XC* in Specify Vector, keep Specify Point at (0, 0, 0), and type Start 0 and End 180.
Click *OK*.

image::tutorial-AO-01-wall.png[The wall of the casing, revolved half a turn about XC]

=== 2. The edge

Click *Extrude*, keep *Single Curve*, and click the 10 curves of the edge: the inner curve's four again, the four of the curve 1.5 below it, and the two lines that close its ends.
Most of these curves now lie on a face or an edge of the wall as well. Press and hold MB1 on the curve, as the book's note says, and choose the sketch curve in QuickPick.

Keep Boolean *None*.
Type 12.7 mm (0.5 in) as the length and tick *Reversed* in FreeCAD's panel, which turns the extrusion to -ZC.
Click *OK*.

The edge becomes a body of its own.
With Unite it would touch the wall only along a line, which would leave two solids in one body, so the dialog does not allow it.

image::tutorial-AO-02-edge.png[The edge extruded down from the sketch plane]

=== 3. The edge on the other side

Make the edge's body the work body, select its Extrude in the Part Navigator, and click *Mirror Feature* (Home › Pattern).
Choose the plane *XZ*, the plane through the casing's centre line, and press MB2.

image::tutorial-AO-03-mirror.png[The edge mirrored to the other side of the casing]

=== 4. The flange

Click *Revolve* again and, with *Single Curve*, click the 4 sides of the flange at the casing's smaller opening.
Two of them lie on lines of the wall and of the edge: QuickPick lists both, and you choose the flange's.
Choose *XC*, keep (0, 0, 0), and type Start 0 and End 180.
The Boolean is *Unite*, with the wall, by itself; click *OK*.

image::tutorial-AO-04-casing.png[The lower casing with its flange]

Save the part.

== What differs from NX

* NX's Revolve and Extrude start with the Section selection. In FreeCAD a feature without a sketch first offers the sketch planes; picking a curve instead closes that list.
* NX's Extrude limits give the direction. Here the length and the direction are set in FreeCAD's panel under the NX dialog: 12.7 mm with Reversed is NX's 0.5 in along -ZC.
* The book mirrors the edge with Edit > Transform > Mirror Through a Plane and Copy, which makes an unparametrised copy. NX-Style uses Mirror Feature, so the copy follows the edge when it changes.
* Mirror Feature mirrors a feature inside its body: the edge's body then holds both edges, two separate solids.
