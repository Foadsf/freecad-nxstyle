= Tutorial T: A guide bush documented to ISO GPS
:description: Technical product documentation in the model: ISO 286 fits, the envelope, ISO 2768 general tolerances, datums and geometrical tolerances as 3D PMI, the inspection plan and its checks, the fit as a stack-up, and the drawing inheriting the PMI.

[horizontal]
Book:: The PMI workflow of Wünsch, Pilz and Vajna, _Siemens NX für Einsteiger – kurz und bündig_, 4th ed., section 6.10 (PMI tab, Rapid Dimension on a cylindrical face, tolerances, the PMI folder); the part is ours
Standards:: ISO 16792 (3D annotation), ISO 286 (fits), ISO 14405 (the envelope), ISO 8015 (independency), ISO 2768 (general tolerances), ISO 1101 and ISO 5459 (geometrical tolerances and datums)
You learn:: PMI › Tolerancing, Rapid Dimension with a tolerance class, the envelope, datums, frames, Inspection Plan, the ISO 8015 trap, a fit as a stack-up, STEP with PMI, Inherit PMI
Sizes:: A flange Ø40 × 8, a body Ø25 × 30 pressed into a housing, a bore Ø16 for a sliding shaft

The bush's body is pressed into a housing (Ø25 k6 in an H7 hole); its shoulder seats on the housing; a Ø16 g6 shaft slides in its bore.

== Step by step

=== 1. The part and its standard

The bush is modelled by script; the exercise is the documentation.
*PMI › Tolerancing*: *ISO GPS*, general tolerances `ISO 2768-mK`.

=== 2. Sizes with their fits

Select the body's cylinder, *PMI › Rapid Dimension*: tolerance class `k6`, *Envelope* ticked, OK: _Ø25.000 k6 (+0.015/+0.002) Ⓔ_.
Then the bore: `H7`, without the envelope for now.

=== 3. Datums and geometrical tolerances

*Datum Feature Symbol* A on the body's cylinder, B on the shoulder.
*Feature Control Frame*: coaxiality Ø0.02 to A on the bore; perpendicularity 0.02 to A on the shoulder.

=== 4. Dimensions without a tolerance of their own

*Rapid Dimension* between the shoulder and the top: 30, nothing typed; the flange 8 ±0.05.
The 30 is covered by ISO 2768-m: ±0.2 (over 6 up to and including 30).

image::tutorial-T-03-pmi.png[The bush with its PMI: fits with the envelope, datums A and B, two frames, the balloon numbers]

=== 5. The inspection plan, and the ISO 8015 trap

*PMI › Inspection Plan* numbers the six characteristics and checks the PMI.
It flags the bore: _H7 is a fit, but under ISO 8015 independency the size controls only two-point sizes, not form_.
A bore within H7 at every section can still be bent or barrel-shaped enough to bind the shaft.
Tick the bore's *Envelope* in the property view and run the plan again: no warnings.

image::tutorial-T-02-plan.png[The inspection plan: characteristics with their limits, where each tolerance comes from, and the checks]

=== 6. The fit as a stack-up

*Tolerance Stackup*, select the bore's PMI dimension, *Add from PMI*; *Add Typed* for the shaft: 16, direction −, class `g6`.
The worst case is a clearance of 0.006 to 0.035 mm, as ISO 286 gives for Ø16 H7/g6.
The stack-up reads the bore's class from the PMI: change it, and the fit follows (xref:tutorials/v.adoc[tutorial V] does).

=== 7. STEP with PMI

*PMI › STEP with PMI*, a file name, OK.
The file holds the bush and its PMI as STEP AP242 data: datums A and B, the two diameters with H7 and k6, the 8 ±0.05 as a size, the 30 as a location, the coaxiality and perpendicularity frames with their datums.
OpenCASCADE reads it back: 2 datum references, 4 dimensions, 2 geometrical tolerances.
The envelopes and the ISO 2768 note are listed as not written: AP242 in OpenCASCADE has no place for them.

image::tutorial-T-06-step.png[The Information window after STEP with PMI: what the file holds, what was not written, and what OpenCASCADE read back]

=== 8. The drawing inherits the PMI

kbd:[Ctrl+Shift+D], *New Sheet*, a top view and a front view at 2:1.
Select each view and click *Inherit PMI*: the diameters with k6 and H7 and Ⓔ, the 30 and the 8 ±0.05, the frames, the datum symbols, and the note _Tolerancing ISO 8015 · General tolerances ISO 2768-mK_.

image::tutorial-T-05-drawing.png[The drawing made from the PMI]

== What differs from NX

* NX places each PMI with a click and stores it in model views; here a PMI is placed beside its face.
* NX has no ISO 8015 check and no inspection plan; NX-Style's come from the PMI's semantics.
* NX writes PMI into STEP AP242 and JT; NX-Style writes STEP AP242 with semantic PMI only (no graphical PMI, no JT).
