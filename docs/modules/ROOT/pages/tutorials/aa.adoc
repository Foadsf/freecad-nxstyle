= Tutorial AA: Composite position, evaluated from CMM data
:description: A cover plate whose four holes are located to the datums by one row of a composite frame and held to each other by the other row; the inspection plan's two rows and two gauges, the rule on the lower row's datums, and the measured holes evaluated against both rows.

[horizontal]
Books:: Cogorno, _Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing for Mechanical Design_, ch. 12 (composite position) and ch. 13 (graphic analysis); Drake, _Dimensioning and Tolerancing Handbook_, ch. 5
Standards:: ASME Y14.5 (composite position tolerancing)
You learn:: Frame Style Composite Frame, PLTZF and FRTZF, the gauges of a composite frame, Analysis › Position Evaluation
Sizes:: A plate 80 × 80 × 10 with four holes Ø5 +0.2/0 on a 40 mm square, 20 mm from datums B and C

A cover plate is bolted on with four screws, and the plate's holes must line up with the tapped holes underneath.
The pattern may sit 0.2 mm off where datums B and C put it.
The holes must stay within 0.04 mm of each other, square to A, because the screws go into a matching pattern.
One composite frame says both things.

The hole layout is that of Cogorno's graphic analysis example (ch. 13), converted to millimetres. The measured data are ours.

== Step by step

=== 1. The plate, its datums and its holes

The plate is made by script. Its datums are A (underside), B (left) and C (front), and its holes are dimensioned 4X Ø5 +0.2/0, all on the four hole faces.
The part's standard is ASME Y14.5.

=== 2. The composite frame

Select the four hole faces, then *PMI › Feature Control Frame*:

* *Position*, Ø ticked, `0.2`, *MMC*, datums `A` `B` `C`;
* *Frame Style* *Composite Frame*, lower row `0.04`, *MMC*, datum `A`.

image::tutorial-AA-01-dialog.png[The Feature Control Frame dialog with the lower row]

The label is a two-row frame with one position symbol: `position | Ø0.200 (M) | A | B | C / Ø0.040 (M) | A`.

=== 3. The inspection plan

*PMI › Inspection Plan* lists each row as its own characteristic:

* *PLTZF*: Ø0.2 at MMC, up to Ø0.4 at LMC, which locates and orients the pattern to A|B|C;
* *FRTZF*: Ø0.04 at MMC, up to Ø0.24 at LMC, which holds the spacing and the orientation to A.

The functional gauge has two gauges.
The first has pins of Ø4.8 on A, B and C.
The second has pins of Ø4.96 that float on A, with no stops for B and C.

image::tutorial-AA-02-plan.png[Two rows, two gauges]

=== 4. The rule on the lower row's datums

Set the frame's *LowerDatumReferences* to `B` in the property view: the plan's check reports an error.
A composite lower row may only repeat the upper row's datums, in their order. They orient the row and never locate it.
Then set it back to `A`.

=== 5. Position Evaluation

Select the frame, then *Analysis › Position Evaluation*.
The table lists the true positions from B and from C, read from the model.
Type the CMM's results into the table:

[cols="1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Hole | From B | From C | Size
| 1 | 19.94 | 20.06 | 5.12
| 2 | 20.08 | 60.08 | 5.16
| 3 | 60.12 | 59.96 | 5.20
| 4 | 60.04 | 19.96 | 5.08
|===

The result is ACCEPT.

* Each hole's bonus is its size less 5.0. With it, the pattern-locating zones are 63 % used.
* The feature-relating zones may slide and turn on A. Fitted to the holes, they are 72 % used.

image::tutorial-AA-03-evaluation.png[Both rows pass]

=== 6. If the lower row could not turn

Double-click the evaluation, then choose *Lower row zones* › *Translate only*.
This is what a lower row repeating A|B would allow.
The same holes now use 103 % of the zones: REJECT.
The datums a composite lower row repeats decide whether this part passes.

image::tutorial-AA-04-pmi.png[The plate with its composite frame]

== What differs from NX

* NX's Feature Control Frame has a composite frame style as well. NX-Style also checks the lower row's datums.
* NX states the requirement but does not evaluate measured data; CMM software does that.
  *Position Evaluation* is NX-Style's.
* STEP AP242 receives the two rows as two position tolerances, because OpenCASCADE cannot link them.
