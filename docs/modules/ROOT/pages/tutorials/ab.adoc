= Tutorial AB: Measurement capability
:description: A bearing plate whose characteristics carry the uncertainty of the method that checks them: the inspection plan gives each TUR and the ISO 14253-1 acceptance limits; two gauge studies, one of them Drake's worked example.

[horizontal]
Books:: Drake, _Dimensioning and Tolerancing Handbook_, ch. 20 (measurement systems analysis) and ch. 25 (gauge repeatability and reproducibility)
Standards:: ISO 14253-1 (decision rules), ANSI/NCSL Z540.3 (test uncertainty ratio), AIAG MSA
You learn:: Measurement uncertainty U on PMI, TUR, guard-banded acceptance limits, Analysis › Gauge Study
Sizes:: A plate 60 × 40 × 12 with a bore Ø20 H7

A tolerance is only as good as the measurement that checks it.
A caliper reading of 12.04 on a 12 ±0.05 thickness does not prove that the part is good.
The inspection method's uncertainty belongs with the characteristic.

The inspection methods' uncertainties are ours. The first gauge study uses the ranges of Drake's worked example.

== Step by step

=== 1. The plate

The plate is made by script. Its datums are A, B and C, the bore is positioned Ø0.2 to A|B|C (RFS, as in Drake's gauge study), and the general tolerances are ISO 2768-mK.

=== 2. The characteristics and their methods

In each PMI dialog, *Measurement uncertainty U* is the expanded uncertainty of the method:

[cols="2,1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Characteristic | U | Method
| Bore Ø20 H7 | 0.002 | bore gauge
| Thickness 12 ±0.05 | 0.01 | micrometer
| Flatness 0.01 of the top | 0.004 | surface plate and indicator
|===

=== 3. The inspection plan

*PMI › Inspection Plan* adds a TUR and the acceptance limits to each characteristic:

----
U 0.002: TUR 5.2:1 (meets 4:1); accept 20.002 to 20.019 (ISO 14253-1)
U 0.010: TUR 5.0:1 (meets 4:1); accept 11.960 to 12.040 (ISO 14253-1)
U 0.004: TUR 1.2:1 (below 4:1: guard band or a better method); accept 0.006 max (ISO 14253-1)
----

A bore reading of 20.020 is inside the tolerance but within U of its limit, so it proves nothing.
The flatness check uses 80 % of its tolerance on uncertainty: accept up to 0.006, or measure it better.

image::tutorial-AB-01-plan.png[TUR and acceptance limits in the inspection plan]

=== 4. Drake's gauge study

Select the position frame, then *Analysis › Gauge Study*: gauge `CMM`, ten parts with two readings each, and spread *3 sigma (Drake ch. 25)*.
The average range is 0.032, so σ = 0.032 / 1.128 = 0.0284 and 3σ = 0.085: 42.6 % of the 0.2 tolerance.
That is over 30 %, so the measurement system is not acceptable, as in the book.
With AIAG's 6σ the share would be twice that.

image::tutorial-AB-02-drake.png[Drake's example: 42.6 %, unacceptable]

=== 5. An air gauge on the bore

The same study on the bore H7 with an air gauge, at 6σ, uses under 7 % of the 0.021 tolerance: acceptable.
The study stays in the Part Navigator beside the PMI it qualifies, and recomputes if the tolerance changes.

== What differs from NX

NX's PMI has no measurement uncertainty, TUR or gauge study; quality systems hold them.
These tools are NX-Style's (xref:inspection.adoc[Inspection]).
The range method of one operator gives repeatability only; studies with several operators (reproducibility) are not implemented.
