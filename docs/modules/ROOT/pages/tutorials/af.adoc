= Tutorial AF: Arbor Press Base
:description: A sketch drawn roughly with Profile, brought to size with geometric constraints and dimensions until it is fully defined, then extruded 60 mm (NX 12 for Engineering Design, 3.5.1).

[horizontal]
Source:: Ming C. Leu, Wenjin Tao, Amir Ghazanfari and Krishna Kolan, _NX 12 for Engineering Design_ (Missouri University of Science and Technology), section 3.5.1
You learn:: Profile with lines and an arc, geometric constraints before dimensions, Equal, Collinear, Tangent, a fully defined sketch, Extrude from a selected sketch
Sizes:: from the book's dimension figure: 130 wide, 190.94 high; the outer sides 125; the top 95 wide with a notch 65 wide and 65 deep; a slot 40 wide ending in a semicircle whose centre is the origin, 35 above the bottom edge; extruded 60 mm

== Step by step

=== 1. A new part and a sketch

kbd:[Ctrl+N] for a new part.
Click *Sketch* on the Home tab, pick the XC-YC plane and press MB2.

=== 2. Profile: lines and one arc

Press kbd:[Z] for *Profile*.
Start at the bottom left and click round the outline: along the bottom to the slot, up its left side, then press kbd:[M] three times to switch to a tangent arc and click the slot's right side.
Where the book clicks *Line* in the Profile dialog, press kbd:[M] twice: FreeCAD would otherwise carry on with a line held tangent to the arc.
Continue with lines down the slot, along the bottom, up the right side, through the notch at the top and down the left side, and click the first point: that closes the outline.
MB2 ends Profile.
Draw it roughly. The constraints bring it to shape.
Each straight line gets Horizontal or Vertical as it is drawn, except the two slanted ones.
FreeCAD has no automatic dimensions to switch off, so the book's note about Continuous Auto Dimensioning does not apply.

=== 3. Geometric constraints first

* *Point on Curve*: pick the Y axis and the arc's centre and click *Point on Curve*, then the X axis and the centre.
* *Equal*: the two slanted lines, then the two long vertical lines.
* The bottom lines: pick both and click *Collinear*, then *Equal*.
* *Tangent*: the arc and the slot's sides, where it is missing. Profile made the first one; the line after the arc needs it.
* The book makes the slot's two short sides *Equal*. Here FreeCAD reports that as redundant, because the tangency and the centre on the X axis already make them equal, so leave it out.
* The two short top lines: *Collinear* and *Equal*, as at the bottom. The notch's two sides are then equal too.

=== 4. Dimensions, biggest first

Click *Distance* with the picks selected and type each value: the height 190.94, the width 130, the right side 125, the top 95, the notch 65 wide and 65 deep, the slot 40, and the centre 35 above the bottom.
The Dialog panel then says *Fully constrained* and the sketch turns green.

image::tutorial-AF-01-constrained.png[The Arbor Press Base sketch, fully constrained]

=== 5. Extrude 60 and save

kbd:[Ctrl+Q] finishes the sketch.
Select the sketch, press kbd:[X], type 60 and press MB2.
Save with kbd:[Ctrl+S].

== What differs from NX

* FreeCAD has no collinear constraint. NX-Style's *Collinear* puts the ends of one line on the other line; both bottom lines are horizontal already, so one end is enough and only one point-on-curve constraint is added.
* NX places all the dimensions first and then edits their values. FreeCAD asks for each value as the dimension is made, so the replay types them biggest first, as the book recommends.
* FreeCAD's *Profile* switches to an arc with kbd:[M], and back to a plain line with kbd:[M] twice more. NX has Line and Arc buttons in the Profile toolbar.
* FreeCAD rejects a constraint that is already implied, and reports it as redundant.
