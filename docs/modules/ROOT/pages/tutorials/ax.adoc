= Tutorial AX: Impeller Upper Casing
:description: The lower casing of tutorial AO turned into the upper one with Edit Parameters, and a manhole added: a square tube and an opening through the wall (NX 12 for Engineering Design, exercise 4.10.3).

[horizontal]
Source:: Ming C. Leu, Wenjin Tao, Amir Ghazanfari and Krishna Kolan, _NX 12 for Engineering Design_ (Missouri University of Science and Technology), exercise 4.10.3 (Impeller Upper Casing). It starts from the lower casing of xref:tutorials/ao.adoc[tutorial AO].
You learn:: Edit Parameters of a Revolve and of an Extrude, a Datum Plane at an offset, a sketch on it, Extrude with Unite, Extrude (Subtract) of some of a sketch's curves, Show
Sizes:: the lower casing's, in inches, typed in millimetres. The manhole is ours (see below).

The book gives the upper casing the lower casing's dimensions and leaves the manhole to the reader: it should be big enough to see the impeller blades and to put a hand inside to clean it.
Here it is a square tube 5 × 5 in with 0.5 in walls, its top 3 in above the casing (z = 18 in), over the middle of the wall's straight part, and a 4 × 4 in opening through the wall.
The tube ends in the middle of the wall's thickness (z = 14.75 in): ending it on the wall's inner face would leave a tangent contact that the Unite cannot make.

The replay runs tutorials AG and AO first and starts from the part AO saved.

== Step by step

=== 1. The lower casing, turned over

Open `Impeller_lower_casing` and save it as `Impeller_upper_casing`.

Select the wall's Revolve in the Part Navigator and click *Edit Parameters*.
Keep Start 0, type End −180, and click *OK*: the wall is now above the split plane.
Do the same for the flange's Revolve.

Select the edge's Extrude, click *Edit Parameters*, untick *Reversed* in FreeCAD's panel and click *OK*.
The edge now runs up from the split plane, and its mirrored copy follows by itself.

=== 2. The manhole

Select the XC-YC plane in the Part Navigator and choose *Datum* > *Datum Plane*. Type 457.2 mm (18 in) in the offset along Z and press MB2.
Select the datum plane and click *Sketch*.
Draw two squares with *Rectangle* (kbd:[R]), both centred over the middle of the wall's straight part: 5 × 5 in and 4 × 4 in. Then kbd:[Ctrl+Q].

Click *Extrude*, click a side of a square, choose *Unite*, and type 82.55 mm (3.25 in).
Tick *Reversed* so that it runs down to the casing, then click *OK*: the tube.

FreeCAD hides a sketch once an Extrude uses it, and a hidden curve cannot be picked.
Select the sketch in the Part Navigator and click *Show* (View tab).
Click *Extrude (Subtract)*, set the Curve Rule to *Single Curve* and pick the inner square's four lines; QuickPick lists them where they lie on the tube.
Type 127 mm (5 in) and click *OK*: the opening through the wall.

Save the part.

== What differs from NX

* NX keeps a sketch visible after an Extrude uses it; FreeCAD hides it, so it has to be shown again before its curves can be picked.
* Right after a selection, Ctrl+Shift+K (NX's Show) and Ctrl+B (Hide) did nothing in the replay until FreeCAD had updated its commands (about 1.5 s); the View tab's Show button worked at once. Both keys now work right after a pick (PR #36); the replay still uses the button.
