= Tutorial AE: One Extrude, every way
:description: New, Sketch and Extrude with Boolean None (a new body), Unite and Subtract, then edit, cancel, undo and reopen: the acceptance case of the Extrude command.

[horizontal]
Source:: Our design. It is the acceptance case of NX-Style's Extrude command (xref:modeling.adoc#extrude[Extrude]), not a book exercise
You learn:: the Extrude dialog's Boolean list, what Cancel and Esc leave behind (nothing), one undo step per command, Edit Parameters from the MB3 menu and the ribbon
Sizes:: a 40 × 30 × 20 block; an 8 mm radius boss 10 high on top; an 8 × 6 pocket 5 deep in the top; a separate 6 mm radius cylinder 15 long, 100 mm away

== Step by step

=== 1. The block: Boolean None (a new body)

kbd:[Ctrl+N], then kbd:[X] with nothing selected.
Pick the XC-YC plane and draw a 40 × 30 rectangle from the origin (kbd:[R]).
kbd:[Ctrl+Q] opens the Extrude dialog.
The part has no solid yet, so the Boolean is *None*: a new body.
Type 20 for the distance and press MB2.

image::tutorial-AE-01-new-body.png[The Extrude dialog with Boolean None]

=== 2. The boss: Unite

Select the top face and press kbd:[X]. The sketch opens on the face.
Draw an 8 mm radius circle in the middle (kbd:[O]) and press kbd:[Ctrl+Q].
The Boolean is now *Unite*, because the part has a solid.
*Intersect* is in the list but greyed out. Choosing it anyway changes nothing, and the dialog says why.
Type 10 and press MB2.

image::tutorial-AE-02-unite.png[The Extrude dialog with Boolean Unite]

=== 3. The pocket: Subtract

Select the top face again and press kbd:[X].
Draw an 8 × 6 rectangle near a corner, then kbd:[Ctrl+Q].
Choose *Subtract* in the Boolean list. The preview changes from adding material to cutting it, and no undo step is added.
Type 5 and press MB2. The pocket cuts into the part.

=== 4. A separate body: Boolean None

kbd:[X] with nothing selected. Pick the XC-ZC plane and draw a 6 mm radius circle 100 mm from the part.
The Boolean *Unite* is inferred, but the circle misses the part.
The dialog shows the error and OK is unavailable, so MB2 does nothing.
Choose *None*, type 15 and press MB2.
The cylinder is a second Body. The first one is unchanged.

image::tutorial-AE-03-new-body.png[A Unite that misses the part, changed to None (a new body)]

=== 5. Edit, Cancel, edit, OK

MB3 on the first extrude in the Part Navigator, then *Edit Parameters*.
Type 35: the preview grows.
Press *Cancel*. The distance is 20 again, and the undo list is unchanged.
Select the extrude again and click *Edit Parameters* on the ribbon. Type 25 and press MB2.
The boss and the pocket move up with the top face.
This edit is one undo step, *Edit Extrude*.

=== 6. Undo, Redo, reopen

kbd:[Ctrl+Z] takes back the edit.
A second kbd:[Ctrl+Z] takes back the whole new-body extrude: the Body, its binder and the Pad go together.
kbd:[Ctrl+Y] twice brings both back.

Type `Extrude (Subtract)` into the Command Finder and press Enter. The plane pick appears; *Cancel* it. Nothing is added to the part or to the undo list.

Save (kbd:[Ctrl+S]), close and reopen the part.
Then *Edit Parameters* on the pocket: the Boolean reads *Subtract*. Type 12 and press kbd:[Esc], which leaves the pocket as it was.

== What differs from NX

* The dialog has two parts: the NX command box (section, body, Boolean, errors) above FreeCAD's own Pad or Pocket panel (limits, direction, draft).
* Subtract cuts into the part by default. NX keeps the sketch normal for every Boolean, so there you reverse the direction.
* Unite and Subtract work on the Body that holds the sketch. NX lets you pick the target body.
* A sketch made inside Extrude stays as its own undo steps. Undoing the extrude leaves the sketch.
