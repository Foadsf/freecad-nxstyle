= Tutorial AN: Arbor Press Plate
:description: A plate made with NX's Block, a datum plane offset from its top face, a datum axis through two corners, and six simple holes drilled from points on the top face (NX 12 for Engineering Design, 4.2.1, 4.3 and 4.5.1).

[horizontal]
Source:: Ming C. Leu, Wenjin Tao, Amir Ghazanfari and Krishna Kolan, _NX 12 for Engineering Design_ (Missouri University of Science and Technology), sections 4.2.1 (Model a Block), 4.3 (Reference Features) and 4.5.1 (General Hole). The book builds the plate in three visits; here they follow one another.
You learn:: Block with Origin and Edge Lengths, Fit and Trimetric, Datum Plane at an offset, Datum Axis through two points, Hole with its points sketched on a face
Sizes:: the book's, in inches, typed in millimetres. A block 65 × 85 × 20 with its origin at (0, 0, 0). A datum plane 15 above the top face. Six simple holes Ø8, 25 deep with a 118° tip, at (11.25, 10), (32.5, 23.5), (53.75, 10), (11.25, 75), (32.5, 61.5) and (53.75, 75).

The book's figure picks the datum axis's points without coordinates: a bottom corner and the opposite top corner of the front face.
Here they are (65, 0, 0) and (0, 0, 20), so the axis is the front face's diagonal.
The plate is 20 thick, so holes 25 deep go through it.

== Step by step

=== 1. The block

kbd:[Ctrl+N], then *Block* (Home › Feature).
In the Block dialog keep *Origin and Edge Lengths*, leave XC, YC and ZC at 0, and type Length 65, Width 85 and Height 20.
Click *OK*.

kbd:[Ctrl+F] fits the view, and kbd:[Home] turns it to Trimetric (NX: MB3 > Orient View > Trimetric).
Save the part as `Arborpress_plate`.

image::tutorial-AN-01-block.png[The block, 65 by 85 by 20]

=== 2. A datum plane and a datum axis

Select the block's top face and choose *Datum* > *Datum Plane*.
FreeCAD attaches the plane to the face; in the Dialog panel type 15 in the offset along Z and press MB2.

Select two corners of the block, here (65, 0, 0) and (0, 0, 20), and choose *Datum* > *Datum Axis*.
FreeCAD takes the axis through the two points, as NX's Inferred type does; press MB2.

image::tutorial-AN-02-datums.png[The datum plane above the top face and the datum axis along the front face's diagonal]

=== 3. The holes

Select the top face and click *Hole*.
NX-Style starts a sketch on the face, which is NX's Sketch Section in the Hole dialog's Position group.
Click *Point* (Sketch › Curve), then for each of the six points type XC and YC in the fields beside the pointer, pressing kbd:[Enter] after each.
Press MB2, then kbd:[Ctrl+Q].

The Hole dialog opens.
Keep the simple hole, and type Diameter 8, Depth 25 and the tip angle 118°.
Press MB2.

image::tutorial-AN-03-plate.png[The plate with its six holes]

Save the part.

== What differs from NX

* NX's Point Dialog opens as its own window. In NX-Style's Block dialog its XC, YC and ZC are fields of the dialog itself.
* NX's Datum Plane dialog offers the offset as soon as you pick the face. FreeCAD attaches the plane to the face and takes the offset as the attachment's Z offset.
* NX places a hole's points with the Point Dialog in the hole's sketch. FreeCAD's Hole drills at the sketch's points, and the points are typed in the on-view fields, which are NX's XC and YC boxes.
* FreeCAD's Hole always subtracts from its body; NX's Hole has a Boolean group.
