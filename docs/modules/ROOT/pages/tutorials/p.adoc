= Tutorial P: Waffle canopy
:description: A ribbed canopy from one profile curve: mirror, two arches, a four-sided surface, contour slices offset into ribs and extruded.

[horizontal]
Video:: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q0rWnXPHeFQ[Grasshopper Tutorial For beginners] (YouTube, channel Rhino Grasshopper)
Application:: xref:algorithmic-modeling.adoc[Algorithmic Modeling] (Katydid)
You learn:: Mirroring in a plane, merging points into a curve, a surface from boundary curves, contours, offset and ruled surfaces, extruding by a computed thickness
Sizes:: Millimetres: a 58 × 70 profile, a 90 mm span, 13 ribs 4 mm deep

The canopy grows from one curve.
Its mirror gives the far side, two arches close it at the bottom and the top, a surface spans the four, and parallel slices of that surface become the ribs.

== Step by step

=== 1. The profile

The video draws half of the profile in Rhino's Front view and references it into a *Curve* parameter.
Katydid cannot reference a FreeCAD sketch yet, so the profile is drawn on the canvas instead: five *Construct Point* components, their X and Z typed with right-click › *Set value…*, all wired into one *Interpolate* with kbd:[Shift] held, so they arrive in order.

image::tutorial-P-01-profile.png[Five points interpolated into the profile curve]

=== 2. The Bottom and Top points

Two more Construct Points stand in for the two Rhino points: *Bottom* (X 14, Y 45) sets the mirror plane and the bulge at the base, *Top* (X 44, Y 20, Z 84) the height and depth of the top arch.
Sliders move them, as the Gumball does in Rhino.

image::tutorial-P-02-points.png[Bottom and Top points driven by sliders]

=== 3. Mirror

*Mirror* with the profile in *G*, the Bottom point in *P* and the normal *N* typed `0, 1, 0`: the mirror plane is the XZ plane through the Bottom point.
Grasshopper builds that plane with an *XZ Plane* component; Katydid's Mirror takes the point and the normal itself.
Dragging *Half span* now widens or narrows the canopy.

image::tutorial-P-03-mirror.png[The profile and its mirror image, 90 mm apart]

=== 4. End points

Four *Evaluate Curve* components with *t* = 0 and *t* = 1 give the start and end of both curves (Grasshopper's *End Points*; Katydid's *t* always runs from 0 to 1).

=== 5. The two arches

* *Bottom arch*: the profile's start, the Bottom point and the mirror's start, Shift-wired into an Interpolate in that order. Several wires into one input merge their data in order, as Grasshopper's *Merge* does.
* *Top arch*: the same through the Top point *projected* onto the mirror plane. Two *Deconstruct* components and a Construct Point take the Top point's X and Z and the Bottom point's Y.

image::tutorial-P-04-arches.png[The bottom and top arches closing the outline]

=== 6. The surface

The video spans the four curves with *Edge Surface*, which Katydid does not have yet.
A *Loft* through three sections stands in: the profile, a middle section from the Bottom point through the profile's midpoint to the projected Top point, and the mirror.
It passes through both arches at their middle, where the bulge is.

image::tutorial-P-05-surface.png[The canopy surface lofted through three sections]

=== 7. Contours

*Contour* the surface along Y: *N* typed `0, 1, 0`, and *D* a *Series* of plane offsets.
The span is the *Distance* between the two start points; an *Expression* divides it by the *Count* slider (12), and an *Addition* gives count + 1 planes: 13 ribs.

TIP: The video subtracts 0.001 from the spacing so the last slice is not lost on the edge. Katydid's Contour cuts a fine mesh, where a plane that close to the edge splits into fragments, so the first and last planes sit 1 mm inside instead.

image::tutorial-P-06-contours.png[Thirteen contour curves across the surface]

=== 8. Rib strips

*Offset Curve* moves each contour by the *Rib depth* (4 mm) in its own plane.
*Ruled Surface* spans each contour and its offset, both inputs Flattened (right-click › *Flatten*), so the curves pair up one to one.

image::tutorial-P-07-strips.png[Flat strips between the contours and their offsets]

=== 9. Thickness

*Multiplication* of the spacing and a *Thickness ratio* slider (0.3) gives the rib thickness, *Vector XYZ* turns it into a Y vector, and *Extrude* gives the strips their thickness: 13 solid ribs.

image::tutorial-P-08-ribs.png[Thirteen solid ribs]

=== 10. Bake

Select the Extrude and click *Home › Algorithm › Bake*: the ribs become 13 Part solids in the Part Navigator.
Katydid has no *Custom Preview* colours yet, and there is nothing to internalise, since the profile is on the canvas.

image::tutorial-P-09-baked.png[The baked ribs]
