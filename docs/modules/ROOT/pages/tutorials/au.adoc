= Tutorial AU: Impeller, the solid
:description: Tutorial AH's blade sketch made into an impeller: a Cone, the blade extruded and united, a circular Pattern Feature of five blades, and two holes (NX 12 for Engineering Design, 4.7.5).

[horizontal]
Source:: Ming C. Leu, Wenjin Tao, Amir Ghazanfari and Krishna Kolan, _NX 12 for Engineering Design_ (Missouri University of Science and Technology), section 4.7.5 (Impeller). It continues the blade sketch of section 3.5.3, xref:tutorials/ah.adoc[tutorial AH].
You learn:: Cone with a typed point and a vector, Extrude with Unite, Pattern Feature with a circular layout, Cylinder with Subtract
Sizes:: the book's, in inches, typed in millimetres. A cone along −XC from (14, 0, 0), Ø15 to Ø8, 16.25 long. The blade extruded 12 along ZC. Five blades, 72° apart about XC. A hole Ø4 × 16 into the large end and Ø0.275 × 0.25 into the small end.

The replay runs tutorial AH first and opens the part it saved.

== Step by step

=== 1. The cone

Open `Impeller_impeller`, the part of tutorial AH.
Click *Cone*, keep *Diameters and Height* and choose *−XC*.
Type the point (355.6, 0, 0) mm, which is (14, 0, 0) in.
Then type Base Diameter 381, Top Diameter 203.2 and Height 412.75 mm (15, 8 and 16.25 in).
Click *OK*.

=== 2. The blade

Click *Extrude* and click one of the blade's arcs: with the Curve Rule on Inferred Curves the pick takes the whole closed outline.
Choose *Unite*, type 304.8 mm (12 in) and click *OK*.

=== 3. Five blades

Select the Extrude and click *Circular Pattern* (Home › Pattern).
Choose the X axis, 5 occurrences over 360° (72° apart), and press MB2.

=== 4. The holes

Click *Cylinder*, choose *−XC*, type the point (14, 0, 0) in, and type Diameter 4 and Height 16. Choose *Subtract*, and click *OK*.
Click *Cylinder* again, choose *XC*, type the point (−2.25, 0, 0) in at the small end, and type Diameter 0.275 and Height 0.25. Choose *Subtract*, and click *OK*.

Save the part.

== What differs from NX

* NX has one Pattern Feature with a Layout list. FreeCAD has separate buttons; the circular one is *Circular Pattern*.
* The book places the two holes "on the side" of the cone. Here their points are typed at the cone's two ends.
