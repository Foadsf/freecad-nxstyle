= Tutorial X: Locating a laser module with two pins
:description: Opto-mechanics: a laser module located on an optical bench by two dowel pins, analysed from its PMI (fit, translation, rotation, pointing error at 2 m) and improved step by step: a slot, a wider pin base, finer ISO 286 classes, and a check 10 K warm.

[horizontal]
Books:: Drake, _Dimensioning and Tolerancing Handbook_, ch. 24 (pinned interfaces); Fischer, _Mechanical Tolerance Stackup and Analysis_, ch. 15 (the lever of an angle error); the module is ours
You learn:: Analysis › Pin Location from PMI; why two tight holes do not assemble; what a slot does; pin base and ISO 286 class against pointing error; aluminium on steel at another temperature
Sizes:: Ø6 pins pressed into a steel bench; an aluminium laser module with Ø6 holes; the beam must hit a target 2 m away within ±0.15 mm

The module (Aluminum-6061-T6, 23.6 µm/m/K) sits on two pins in the bench (Steel-1C45, 11 µm/m/K).
Its beam leaves along the line of the pins: if the module turns by α about the vertical, the spot moves by 2000 tan α on the target.

== Step by step

=== 1. The layout and its PMI

The bench, the module and the beam are made by script, the materials assigned, and the PMI added as in xref:tutorials/t.adoc[tutorial T]: the holes Ø6 G6 (+0.004 to +0.012), the pins Ø6 h6 (0 to −0.008).
Holes and pins are 80 mm apart, each spacing ±0.005.

=== 2. Pin Location: two holes do not assemble

Select the four PMI dimensions, *Analysis › Pin Location*.
Type the spacing tolerances (0.005), the distance to the target (2000) and the pointing limit (0.15); OK.

The worst-case clearance is −0.006 mm: the holes leave 0.004 mm, but the two spacings may differ by 0.010 mm (Drake 24.2).
Some modules would not go on.
The materials' expansion coefficients are already filled in.

image::tutorial-X-01-two-holes.png[Two holes G6 on pins h6: does not assemble]

=== 3. A hole and a slot

The second hole becomes a slot 6 G6 wide and 7 long along the line of centres; its width gets a PMI dimension with G6, and the analysis takes it as its second feature.
Double-click the analysis and type the slot length, 7.

It assembles: the slot takes the spacings out of the fit (Drake 24.12).
The module can shift 0.010 mm and turn 0.25 mrad: 0.50 mm on the target, over the ±0.15 mm limit.

image::tutorial-X-02-slot.png[A hole and a slot: assembles, but the beam may miss by 0.5 mm]

=== 4. A wider pin base

The pins and the hole and slot move to 200 mm apart.
The PMI and the analysis follow the model: the same clearances now turn the module 0.10 mrad, 0.20 mm on the target. Still over.

=== 5. Finer classes

The holes and the slot become G5, the pins h5, in the PMI's *Fit*.
Translation 0.007 mm, rotation 0.070 mrad, pointing 0.14 mm: within ±0.15 mm.

=== 6. Assembled 10 K warm

Double-click the analysis and type 10 K.
Aluminium on steel, 200 mm apart: the spacings part by 0.0252 mm (Drake 24.10).
With two holes, each would have to grow by that much, adding 0.0126 mm of translation and 0.126 mrad of rotation, more than the budget.
The slot takes it up in its length; translation and rotation stay as they were.

== What differs from NX

* NX has no pin location analysis; a variation analysis add-on does this kind of study there.
* The model changes (the slot, the wider base) are made by script, and the PMI is kept on the same features by the script; in NX the PMI would follow the edited features.
