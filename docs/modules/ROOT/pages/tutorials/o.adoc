= Tutorial O: Grasshopper basics, sphere and cylinder
:description: The first steps of visual programming on the Katydid canvas: a sphere, sliders, two spheres, Bake, and a cylinder exercise.

[horizontal]
Video:: Bastian Wibranek, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cN6XuHxrfxU[Rhino 3D and Grasshopper - Basics Tutorial] (YouTube)
Application:: xref:algorithmic-modeling.adoc[Algorithmic Modeling] (Katydid)
You learn:: Opening the canvas, adding components by name, sliders, wiring, two results from two inputs, Bake, warnings, saving
Sizes:: Millimetres: a sphere of radius 7.5 and one of 3, 40 mm apart; a cylinder 5 × 8

The video teaches Grasshopper inside Rhino.
Here the same steps run on Katydid's canvas inside FreeCAD NX-Style: Katydid plays Grasshopper's part, FreeCAD plays Rhino's.

== Step by step

=== 1. Open the canvas

*Application › Algorithmic Modeling*.
The canvas opens as a floating window over the graphics window, as Grasshopper's floats over Rhino's viewports.

Grasshopper's *Display › Draw Full Names* has no counterpart: Katydid writes each component's short name on it and shows the full name, every input and its type in the tooltip.

=== 2. A sphere

Double-click the empty canvas, type `Sphere` and press kbd:[Enter].
A red preview sphere appears at the origin in the graphics window: the preview is not a document object yet.

image::tutorial-O-01-sphere.png[A Sphere component and its preview at the origin]

NOTE: Grasshopper's Sphere also has a *Base* (B) input for its position; Katydid's Sphere sits at the origin, so the next step moves it.

=== 3. Move it

In the video a point drawn in Rhino is referenced into the sphere's *Base* (*Set one Point*) and dragged with the Gumball.
Katydid cannot reference a FreeCAD point yet, so a *Move* after the sphere does the moving:

. Double-click, `Move`, kbd:[Enter].
. Drag a wire from the sphere's output *S* to Move's *G*.
. Double-click, `Number Slider`, kbd:[Enter]. Double-click the slider to name it `X` and give it the range −50 to 50, value 20.
. Wire the slider into Move's *X*.
. Right-click the sphere and switch its preview off, so only the moved sphere shows.

Drag the slider and the sphere follows, live.

image::tutorial-O-02-move.png[Sphere, Move and an X slider: the sphere 20 mm along X]

=== 4. A slider for the radius

Add a second Number Slider, name it `Radius`, range 0 to 10, and wire it into the sphere's *R*.
At 7.5 the sphere's volume is 1767.1 mm³, as 4/3 π r³ gives.

image::tutorial-O-03-radius.png[A Radius slider wired into the sphere]

=== 5. Two spheres

The video references two points (*Set Multiple Points*) and copies the radius slider.
Here a second X slider (−20) goes into Move's *X* and a second radius slider (3) into the sphere's *R*, each wire dragged with kbd:[Shift] held so it is added, not swapped.
Lists pair up item by item: radius 7.5 at x = 20 and radius 3 at x = −20.

image::tutorial-O-04-two-spheres.png[Two spheres from two radius sliders and two X sliders]

=== 6. Bake

*Home › Algorithm › Bake* (or *Bake* on the canvas) turns what is previewed into ordinary Part features.
`Move 0` and `Move 1` appear in the Part Navigator and stay when the canvas is closed.

image::tutorial-O-05-bake.png[The two spheres baked into the Part Navigator]

=== 7. Exercise: a cylinder

Clear the canvas, add a *Cylinder* and two sliders, `Radius` 5 and `Length` 8, and wire them into *R* and *L*.
Its volume is 628.3 mm³ (π r² h).
The baked spheres stay in the document, grey; the cylinder is still a red preview.

image::tutorial-O-06-cylinder.png[A Cylinder with Radius and Length sliders]

TIP: In Grasshopper, typing `5` into the search box makes a slider at 5. Katydid's search finds components only, so the slider is added and then set.

=== 8. Warnings

A component that misses an input turns orange.
Add a *Move* with nothing in *G*: it says "Input parameter G failed to collect data" when you hover it.
Wire the cylinder into *G* and it is grey again.

=== 9. Save

Grasshopper keeps its definition in a `.gh` file beside Rhino's `.3dm`, and both have to be opened together.
Katydid saves the canvas inside the part: one *Save*, and the `.FCStd` holds the baked spheres and the canvas (the *Katydid* object in the Part Navigator).

image::tutorial-O-07-saved.png[The saved part with its canvas]

== The replay

Every step above is done by the xref:testing.adoc#tutorial-replay[tutorial replay] (`--tutorials O`): it double-clicks the canvas and types each name, drags each wire between grips, types the slider values into the slider's dialog, and checks every result by its volume and position.
