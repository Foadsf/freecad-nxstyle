= Tutorial AQ: Hexagonal Screw
:description: A hexagon-head screw from NX primitives, a sketched hexagon, a Sphere intersected with the head and a Detailed Thread on the shank (NX 12 for Engineering Design, 4.7.1).

[horizontal]
Source:: Ming C. Leu, Wenjin Tao, Amir Ghazanfari and Krishna Kolan, _NX 12 for Engineering Design_ (Missouri University of Science and Technology), section 4.7.1 (Hexagonal Screw).
You learn:: Cylinder at the centre of a face, Polygon, Extrude as a body of its own, Static Wireframe for picking hidden edges, Sphere with Intersect and Select Body, Thread (Detailed)
Sizes:: the book's, in inches, typed in millimetres. Shank Ø0.25 × 1.5, step Ø0.387 × 0.0156, hexagon side 0.246 extruded 0.1876, a cylinder Ø0.387 × 0.1875 inside the head, a Sphere Ø0.55. The thread is NX's default for the shank, 1/4-20 UNC.

== Step by step

=== 1. The shank and the step

kbd:[Ctrl+N] and save the part as `Impeller_hexa-bolt`.
Click *Cylinder*, choose *ZC*, keep the point at (0, 0, 0), and type Diameter 0.25 and Height 1.5 (6.35 and 38.1 mm).
Click *OK*.

Click *Cylinder* again and type Diameter 0.387 and Height 0.0156 (9.8298 and 0.39624 mm).
In Specify Point choose *Arc/Ellipse/Sphere Center* and click the shank's top face: the point is its centre.
Choose *Unite* and click *OK*.

image::tutorial-AQ-01-cylinders.png[The shank with the thin step cylinder on top]

=== 2. The hexagon head

Select the step's top face and click *Sketch*: the sketch's origin is the circle's centre.
Press kbd:[P] for *Polygon*.
FreeCAD asks for the number of sides: type 6 and click OK.
Type the centre (0, 0) in the fields beside the pointer, then the corner: length 6.2484 mm (0.246 in) at angle 0.
A regular hexagon's circumradius equals its side length, so this is NX's Side Length 0.246 with Rotation 0.
Press MB2, then kbd:[Ctrl+Q].

Click *Extrude*, click a side of the hexagon, keep Boolean *None* and type 4.76504 mm (0.1876 in).
Click *OK*.
The head is a body of its own, which the Intersect below needs.

=== 3. The rounded head

Switch to *Static Wireframe* (View tab): the next two picks are edges inside the head.
Click *Cylinder*, type Diameter 0.387 and Height 0.1875, and with *Arc/Ellipse/Sphere Center* click the step's top edge.
Keep Boolean *None*, and click *OK*.

Click *Sphere* (*Center Point and Diameter*). With *Arc/Ellipse/Sphere Center* click the bottom edge of that cylinder, and type Diameter 0.55 (13.97 mm).
Choose *Intersect*, choose the hexagonal head in *Select Body*, and click *OK*.
Switch back to *Shaded with Edges*.

image::tutorial-AQ-02-screw.png[The screw with its head rounded by the sphere]

=== 4. The thread

Click *Thread* (Home › Feature), choose *Detailed*, keep *Right Hand*, and click the shank.
The dialog fills in 1/4-20 UNC for the 0.25 in shank: major 6.35, minor 4.975, pitch 1.27, angle 60° and length 38.1 mm.
Keep the values and click *OK*.

image::tutorial-AQ-03-thread.png[The finished screw with its thread]

Save the part.

== What differs from NX

* NX 12 draws the hexagon with Insert > Curve > Polygon, a curve outside sketches. Here, as in NX 2212, the Polygon is drawn in a sketch on the face.
* FreeCAD's Polygon takes a corner, not a side length. For a hexagon the two are equal.
* FreeCAD asks for the number of sides in a dialog of its own before the polygon is drawn.
* The thread is a FreeCAD Subtractive Helix of the thread's profile. See xref:modeling.adoc[Modeling] for the Thread command.
