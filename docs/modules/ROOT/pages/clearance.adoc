= Assembly clearance and volume claims
:description: NX's Assembly Clearance (hard, containment, touching and soft interference, the Clearance Browser) and volume claims for system layouts.

== Assembly Clearance

NX checks an assembly for interferences with *Assembly Clearance*: a clearance set saved with the assembly, run again after every design change, with the results in the Clearance Browser.
FreeCAD has only *Part › Check Geometry* and booleans between two objects.
NX-Style adds both NX commands, on the *Assemblies* tab (*Clearance Analysis* group) and the *Analysis* tab.

*Simple Interference*:: Select two bodies. The Information window says whether they interfere; a hard interference adds a body of the common volume.

*Clearance Analysis*:: With nothing selected, a new clearance set of every solid in the document; with objects selected, of those; with a clearance set selected, that set again.
The Clearance Browser opens:
+
* *Default clearance zone*: pairs closer than this are soft interferences.
* *Perform Analysis* checks every pair; *Ignore touching pairs* leaves out mating faces.
* The interferences, grouped by kind, with the distance, the penetration depth and the interference volume, and their status against the last run: *New*, *Unchanged*, *Changed* or *Gone*.
* *Study Interference* shows only the selected pair and its interference volume (red); *Restore Visibility* brings the rest back.
* *Exclude Pair* adds a rule to the set and runs it again.

image::tutorial-U-03-clearance.png[The Clearance Browser: a hard interference, a containment, two touching pairs and a soft interference]

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Interference | When
| Hard | The objects intersect.
| Containment | One object is completely inside the other.
| Touching | They touch, with no common volume.
| Soft | They do not touch but are closer than the clearance zone.
|===

The set is a document object (*Clearance Set* in the Part Navigator).
Its properties are what NX's Clearance Analysis dialog holds: *Objects* (empty: every solid), *SecondObjects* (two collections: the first checked against the second only), *ClearanceZone*, *IgnoreTouching* and *PairRules*, one rule per line: `Bolt|Plate=exclude` leaves a pair out, `Shaft|Bushing=0.5` gives it its own zone.
Double-click the set to run it again.

The penetration depth is the smallest extent of the interference volume along its principal axes: for two overlapping blocks, how far one has to move out of the other.

== Volume claims

In a high-tech system layout every module owns a volume: the space reserved for it (a space reservation, _ruimteclaim_).
Its parts must stay inside, other modules' parts must stay out, and two claims must not share space, because then one space has been promised twice.
Keep-out claims reserve space that no part may enter: a beam path, a service access, the sweep of a moving part.

*Assemblies › Volume Claim*:: Select the module's parts first: they become the claim's members and the claim starts as their bounding box, a little larger.
Type the owner, the size and corner, and a margin: members keep it to the claim's boundary, other parts keep it to the claim.
*Keep-out* claims have no members.
A claim can also take its shape from another object (its *Source* property), such as a sketch extruded by the system architect.

*Check Volume Claims*:: Every claim against every part, and every claim against every other claim:
+
[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Finding | Means
| OVERLAP | Two claims share space.
| OUTSIDE | A member sticks out of its claim (or into its margin).
| INTRUSION | Another owner's part enters a claim or its margin, or any part enters a keep-out.
| unclaimed | A part belongs to no claim.
|===
+
The offending volumes are drawn in red (*Claim violations*), the claims turn red, and each claim's *Status* and *Findings* properties keep the result.

image::tutorial-U-01-claims.png[Volume claims with a keep-out for the beam path; the cable bracket enters the stage's claim and the beam path]

Claims are translucent boxes in each owner's colour, kept in the *Volume Claims* folder; hide the folder for a clean view of the parts.
NX has no volume claims: its Assembly Clearance checks parts against parts.
