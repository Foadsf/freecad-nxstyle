= Pin location
:description: Analysis › Pin Location: how precisely two dowel pins locate one part on another (fit, translation, rotation, pointing error, temperature), from the PMI of the pins and the holes.

Opto-mechanical and precision modules are located by two dowel pins: a laser on an optical bench, a sensor head on a frame, a lens barrel in a housing.
Whether the parts assemble, and how far the located part can shift and turn, follow from the pins, the holes and their spacings.
*Analysis › Pin Location* (also on the PMI tab) works it out from the part's PMI, worst case, with the method of Drake's _Dimensioning and Tolerancing Handbook_, ch. 24.

image::tutorial-X-01-two-holes.png[Pin Location on two holes G6 and pins h6: the parts do not assemble]

== Using it

Give the pins and the holes their PMI dimensions (xref:pmi.adoc[PMI]), with ISO 286 classes or deviations: a diameter on each pin and on each hole, or a diameter on the hole and a width on the slot.
Select the four PMI dimensions and click *Pin Location*.
Each pin is paired with the nearest hole; the round hole comes first, a slot second.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Field | Meaning
| Hole spacing ±, Pin spacing ± | Tolerances on the distances between the holes and between the pins. The distances themselves are measured between the axes in the model.
| Slot length | Along the line of centres; it must take up the spacing tolerances (and the thermal growth).
| Distance to target, Pointing limit ± | For a located optical axis: where it must hit, and how closely.
| Temperature difference | Assembly temperature minus the reference temperature.
| Expansion | Of the part with the holes and of the part with the pins, read from their materials when the analysis is made.
|===

The result is a feature in the Part Navigator: *Clearance*, *Translation*, *Rotation* (mrad), *Pointing* (mm), the *Verdict* and the *Report*.
It recomputes when the PMI (a class, a deviation) or the model (a spacing) changes. Double-click it to change the fields.

== The method

*Two holes.* The parts assemble when the holes' clearances absorb the difference between the pin spacing d~p~ and the hole spacing d~h~ (Drake 24.2):

....
c = ½ (Øh1 + Øh2 − Øp1 − Øp2) − |dh − dp| ≥ 0
....

The located part turns by the angle of the triangle with sides d~h~, d~p~ and e = ½(Øh~1~ − Øp~1~ + Øh~2~ − Øp~2~) (24.4); about (Øh − Øp)/d~p~ (24.5).
Translation is half the tighter pin's clearance.

*A hole and a slot.* The slot runs along the line of centres, so the spacings leave the fit; only the slot's length has to take them up (24.12).
Translation is the hole's half-clearance; the rotation follows from the pin line with one end in the hole's clearance circle and the other in the slot's clearance band: sin α = (Δ~hole~ + Δ~slot~)/d~p~.

*Temperature.* Parts of different materials assembled ΔT away from the reference temperature change their spacings by d~p~ ΔT |α~1~ − α~2~| (24.10).
With two holes each hole must grow by that much, which adds half of it to the translation and ΔT |α~1~ − α~2~| to the rotation; a slot takes it up in its length.

*Pointing.* An angle error α moves a point D away by D tan α (Fischer, _Mechanical Tolerance Stackup and Analysis_, ch. 15): 0.1 mrad is 0.2 mm on a target 2 m away.

`nxstyle/alignment.py` holds the method; its unit tests cover each formula.
Drake calls 24.5 slightly conservative; with equal spacings 24.4 equals 2 asin(e/2d), which is larger than e/d by about (e/d)³/24, far below any tolerance. The report gives both.

xref:tutorials/x.adoc[Tutorial X] takes a laser module from two holes that do not assemble to a hole and a slot, a wider pin base and finer classes, and checks it 10 K warm.

== What differs from NX

NX has no pin location analysis; a variation analysis add-on (3D tolerance analysis) covers it there.
The analysis is worst case only; Drake's statistical forms are not implemented.
