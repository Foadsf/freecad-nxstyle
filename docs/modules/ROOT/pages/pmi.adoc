= PMI and technical product documentation
:description: 3D PMI to ISO GPS (or ASME Y14.5): ISO 286 fits, the envelope, ISO 2768 general tolerances, datums, geometrical tolerances, the inspection plan, checks, and drawings that inherit the PMI.

NX documents a part in the model: Product and Manufacturing Information (PMI) on the PMI tab, kept in the Part Navigator's PMI folder and inherited by drawings.
FreeCAD has no PMI.
NX-Style adds it, with the semantics a precision-engineering company works to: technical product documentation (TPD) to ISO GPS, or ASME Y14.5 where a customer asks for it.

== The PMI tab

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Button | Does
| *Tolerancing* | The part's standard, *ISO GPS* (the default) or *ASME Y14.5*, and its general tolerances, e.g. `ISO 2768-mK`: what a drawing's title block says. Stored with the part's PMI folder.
| *Rapid Dimension* | A dimension on the model. Select one cylinder (diameter) or two faces (distance) first. The nominal is measured from the model and follows design changes. Give it an ISO 286 *tolerance class* (H7, g6 …: the dialog shows the limits), or upper and lower deviations, the *envelope* Ⓔ (ISO 14405), *theoretically exact*, and a quantity (4X).
| *Feature Control Frame* | A geometrical tolerance on the selected face: characteristic, Ø zone, tolerance, Ⓜ or Ⓛ, up to three datums with modifiers. The same dialog as Drafting's.
| *Datum Feature Symbol* | A datum letter on the selected face.
| *Inspection Plan* | Numbers the characteristics (the balloons on the model) and lists them with their limits, where the tolerance comes from, and how to measure them; the functional gauge sizes; the checks.
| *Tolerance Stackup* | xref:tolerance-analysis.adoc[Tolerance analysis], with *Add from PMI*.
| *Publish Release Package* | xref:release-package.adoc[Release packages].
|===

Each PMI is attached to its faces by a link that FreeCAD's topological naming keeps on the same face.
It is drawn at a constant size with a leader to the face; frames and datum symbols use the same drawing as NX-Style's Drafting dialogs.
Its values are properties: change the *Fit*, the *Envelope* or a tolerance in the property view and everything that uses it follows, the stack-ups too.

== ISO GPS or ASME Y14.5

The two differ where it matters for a fit:

* *ISO 8015 independency* is ISO's default: a size tolerance controls only local two-point sizes, not form.
A fit is only guaranteed with the envelope Ⓔ, a form tolerance, or a general note with `-E` (`ISO 2768-mK-E`).
ASME's Rule #1 puts every size inside an envelope of perfect form at MMC, so Ⓔ is redundant there.
* *ISO 2768* general tolerances cover every dimension without its own tolerance: `ISO 2768-m` gives a 30 mm length ±0.2 (over 6 up to and including 30), a 40 mm one ±0.3.
ASME Y14.5 has no general tolerances.
* ASME does not use I, O and Q as datum letters, and its 2018 edition dropped concentricity and symmetry (ISO keeps coaxiality and symmetry).

== Checks

*Inspection Plan* checks the PMI as an experienced checker would:

* a fit class without the envelope under ISO 8015 (warning), a feature of size without one (note);
* a dimension with no tolerance and no general tolerance note;
* form tolerances with a datum, orientation and run-out without one, run-out or concentricity with Ⓜ, Ⓜ on a plane, a Ø zone on a plane;
* datums referenced but not identified, identified twice, referenced at MMC though not a feature of size;
* an ISO 286 shaft class on a hole or the other way round.

== Functional gauge

For a frame at MMC on a feature of size, the plan gives the gauge element at its virtual condition: a gauge pin of the hole's MMC minus its tolerance, a gauge hole of the pin's MMC plus its tolerance.
A datum feature of size referenced at MMC is simulated at its maximum material boundary, its orientation tolerance's virtual condition.
The unit tests reproduce Cogorno's hole-pattern plate (ch. 13): pins of .250 for the four holes, .500 for datum D.

== Drawings inherit the PMI

In Drafting, select a view and click *Inherit PMI*:

* diameters and distances become TechDraw dimensions on the matching edges, with their ISO class, deviations, Ⓔ and count;
* frames and datum symbols are placed beside their face, if the view shows it edge-on or face-on;
* the sheet gets the tolerancing note: _Tolerancing ISO 8015_, _General tolerances ISO 2768-mK_.

A PMI is placed once per sheet, in the first view that shows it.
Dimensions covered by the general tolerance show no tolerance of their own, as on a drawing.

== What differs from NX

* NX places a PMI with a click in the graphics window and keeps it in model views; here it is placed beside its face, one row per PMI, and there are no model views.
* NX's PMI is exported in STEP AP242 and JT; FreeCAD's STEP export has no PMI. The xref:release-package.adoc[release package] carries it as `pmi/pmi.json` and the inspection plan.
* The checks and the gauge sizes are NX-Style's own; NX has PMI validation through separate checkers.
